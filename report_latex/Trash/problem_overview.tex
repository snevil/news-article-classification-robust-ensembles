We address a multi-class news article classification problem in which each document must be assigned to one of seven predefined topical categories. Each instance consists of textual content in the form of a title and article body, accompanied by auxiliary metadata. The objective is to learn a mapping from articles to categories that maximizes the macro-averaged F1 score, ensuring balanced performance across classes.

The task is particularly challenging due to the characteristics of real-world online news data. Articles are sourced from heterogeneous publishers and are distributed in raw HTML format, resulting in text that contains markup tags, boilerplate elements, and other non-linguistic artifacts. In addition, topical boundaries between categories are often ambiguous, requiring models to distinguish between overlapping semantic content rather than relying on surface-level lexical cues alone.

These properties motivate the evaluation of modeling strategies with increasing representational capacity. While simpler models may capture coarse regularities in the data, more expressive architectures are required to effectively handle semantic ambiguity and contextual dependencies in noisy, HTML-rich textual content.
