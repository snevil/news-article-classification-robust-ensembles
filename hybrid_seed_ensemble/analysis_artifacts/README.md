# Analysis Artifacts – Ensemble & Prediction Agreement Study

This folder contains all artifacts generated by the *Ensemble Analysis and Prediction Agreement Study* notebook.  
These files provide empirical evidence supporting the ensemble design and hard-case routing strategies adopted in the final submissions.

The goal of this analysis is **diagnostic and justificatory**, not direct performance optimization.

---

## Contents Overview

### 1. `overlap_matrix_fixed.txt`

**Description**  
Pairwise prediction agreement matrix across all 16 Transformer models.

Each entry `(i, j)` represents the fraction of samples for which model *i* and model *j* predict the same class label.

**Key properties**
- Values range approximately from **0.85 to 0.92**
- Highest agreement is observed between:
  - same architecture
  - same input length
- Cross-architecture agreement (DeBERTa vs RoBERTa) is consistently lower

**Interpretation**
- Models are **highly stable but not redundant**
- Architectural diversity provides meaningful disagreement
- This validates the use of ensemble strategies

---

### 2. `overlap_cross_arch.txt`

**Description**  
Subset of the overlap matrix restricted to **cross-architecture comparisons** (DeBERTa vs RoBERTa).

**Interpretation**
- Agreement typically lies in the **0.85–0.89** range
- Pairwise disagreement often exceeds **12–15%**
- Confirms that DeBERTa and RoBERTa capture **partially distinct decision boundaries**

This directly motivates **architecture-aware ensembling**.

---

### 3. `pairwise_disagreement.txt`

**Description**  
Pairwise disagreement matrix, defined as:

disagreement(i, j) = 1 - agreement(i, j)



**Interpretation**
- Highlights where ensemble gains are most likely
- Cross-architecture pairs show the highest disagreement
- Intra-seed and intra-architecture disagreement is lower

This confirms that **seed ensembling alone provides diminishing returns** compared to architectural diversity.

---

### 4. `hard_cases.csv`

**Description**  
List of samples classified as *hard cases* under a conservative disagreement-based definition.

A sample is considered a hard case if:

disagreement ≥ 40%



across the 16 models.

**Statistics**
- Hard cases: **1,338 / 20,000**
- Percentage: **≈ 6.7%**

**Interpretation**
- A small subset of samples concentrates most model uncertainty
- These samples are natural candidates for selective routing strategies
- Disagreement rates reach up to **75%**, indicating near-complete architectural disagreement

---

### 5. `submission_majority_vote.csv`

**Description**  
Predictions generated via majority vote across all 16 models.

**Purpose**
- Low-variance diagnostic baseline
- Used for comparison only

**Limitations**
- Does not exploit:
  - confidence differences
  - architectural specialization
  - uncertainty-aware routing

For this reason, majority voting is **not used as a final submission strategy**.

---

## Hard-Case Class Distribution

Hard cases are unevenly distributed across predicted classes (using a fixed RoBERTa backbone):

- Entertainment: ~27%
- General News: ~26%
- International: ~21%
- Business: ~12%
- Remaining classes (Technology, Health, Sports): <15%

**Interpretation**
- Topic ambiguity and editorial overlap play a major role
- Confirms findings from earlier EDA on structural ambiguity and label noise

---

## Summary of Empirical Findings

- Transformer models are **highly stable across random seeds**
- Architectural diversity is the **primary source of useful disagreement**
- Only a small fraction of samples concentrates most uncertainty
- These findings **directly justify**:
  - logit-level ensembling
  - entropy/disagreement-based hard-case routing
  - architecture-aware specialization

All ensemble configurations used in the final submissions are grounded in the empirical evidence documented in this folder.

---

## Notes

- All predictions are loaded from submission-format CSV files to ensure consistency with leaderboard evaluations
- No calibration metrics (e.g., temperature scaling) are reported here, as full-development logits are not available for all models
- This analysis is **supporting evidence**, not a tuning procedure
